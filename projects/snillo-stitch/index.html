01 · Context

A growing lifestyle brand needed a clearer, more scalable online store.

Methodology note

This case study documents a real production redesign. It is grounded in the legacy storefront, current product and content requirements, responsive implementation constraints, and ongoing commerce operations. Design rationale is separated from outcomes that would require dedicated analytics or formal research validation.

SNILLO STITCH had evolved beyond a simple product storefront. As the range expanded across bags, travel goods, fashion, lifestyle products, promotions, and collaborations, the site needed a clearer structure for customers to understand what to browse and where to go next.

The renewal focused on simplifying discovery, strengthening the brand’s friendly lifestyle tone, and creating a reusable commerce system that could support frequent product and campaign updates.

02 · Evidence & Constraints

The design problem was clarity, not simply adding more content.

The store needed to support a broader product mix and ongoing promotional content while remaining easy to maintain inside Cafe24.

Product Range

More categories meant more decisions at the storefront level.

Bags, travel goods, fashion, living products, seasonal launches, and collaborations required a clearer category and content hierarchy.

Operational Requirement

Recurring modules had to be reusable.

New products, best sellers, promotions, collaboration content, and seasonal campaigns needed patterns that could be updated repeatedly.

Platform Constraint

The design had to ship inside a live Cafe24 environment.

Responsive layouts, content blocks, and interaction patterns had to work within the production CMS/theme and ongoing store operations.

11 · Measurement & Validation

The next iteration should be measured around discovery, comparison, and purchase behaviour.

This case study does not attribute unverified conversion gains to the redesign. Instead, these are the signals I would use to evaluate whether the renewed structure is reducing friction.

DiscoveryHomepage → category / PDP click-through

Which modules most effectively move customers from browsing into shopping?

CataloguePLP → PDP progression

Can customers understand the range and reach relevant products without unnecessary friction?

ProductPDP option / cart completion

Where do customers hesitate between product information, option selection, and purchase intent?

ContentPromotion / collaboration engagement

Do branded content modules actually support shopping, or are they being skipped?
