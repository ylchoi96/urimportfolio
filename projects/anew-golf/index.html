Rebuilding ANEW GOLF
for a sharper digital brand experience.

ANEW GOLF is one of the golf fashion brands operated by Q&B International, my former employer. During my time on the company’s online team, I worked on the e-commerce renewal across UI/UX, responsive design, and front-end implementation — reshaping the storefront so brand storytelling and shopping could work together more clearly.

01 · Context

An owned brand needed a storefront that could evolve with the business.

Methodology note

This case study documents a real production redesign. It is grounded in the existing store structure, live product/content requirements, responsive implementation constraints, and day-to-day commerce operations. Design rationale is separated from outcomes that would require dedicated analytics or research validation.

ANEW GOLF already had an active e-commerce site, but the experience had accumulated many promotional, merchandising, and editorial modules over time. The existing interface communicated products more strongly than the brand’s distinctive identity, while navigation and responsive behaviour left room for a clearer shopping journey.

The renewal focused on making ANEW GOLF’s street- and extreme-sport-influenced visual identity more immediate, while creating clearer paths through categories, new products, rankings, product detail, and brand content.

Because ANEW GOLF was an owned brand within Q&B International, the work also had to support ongoing seasonal launches, product updates, campaigns, events, lookbooks, and news — not just a one-time visual refresh.

02 · Evidence & Constraints

Design decisions came from the realities of an operating commerce brand.

As a brand operated by Q&B International, ANEW GOLF had an active content and commerce pipeline: frequent product launches, campaign updates, editorial assets, promotions, and Cafe24 implementation constraints.

Observed Store Structure

Product information was visible, but brand identity was less immediate.

The legacy site supported shopping, yet product modules and content blocks carried more weight than the distinctive ANEW GOLF mood. The key design problem was hierarchy rather than simply adding more content.

Operational Requirement

The store needed to support frequent seasonal and campaign change.

Collections, campaign visuals, recommendations, events, lookbooks, and news changed continuously, so recurring modules needed reusable patterns rather than one-off layouts.

Platform Constraint

Desktop and mobile both had to work inside a live Cafe24 environment.

Navigation, responsive behaviour, content modules, and front-end decisions were shaped by the production CMS/theme and real operational constraints — not only by what looked ideal in a static design file.

03 · Before / After

The redesign changed more than the visual style.

The legacy homepage was organized around a conventional sequence of NEW, CHECK POINT, KEYWORD, product merchandising, review, and social content. The renewal rebuilt that hierarchy around stronger campaign impact, clearer category entry points, product discovery, ranking, editorial content, and ongoing brand storytelling.

01
Visual hierarchy

From many similarly weighted modules to a clearer sequence of campaign, category, product, and editorial content.

02
Product discovery

From fragmented NEW / CHECK POINT / KEYWORD entry points to more direct category, New Arrivals, and Ranking patterns.

03
Brand content

Expanded the experience beyond review and social modules into Lookbook, Event, and News structures that support ongoing brand operations.

04
Responsive experience

Moved from limited mobile adaptation toward a mobile-first responsive hierarchy designed around the browsing patterns of a younger golf audience.

04 · Design Direction

Editorial enough to feel like fashion. Structured enough to shop.

ANEW GOLF draws visual energy from streetwear and extreme-sport culture. The redesign translates that identity into stronger full-screen campaign imagery, bold category moments, and a clearer commerce rhythm: brand impact first, product discovery next, then supporting editorial and brand content.

05 · Homepage

A modular homepage with clearer hierarchy and pacing.

The renewed homepage connects campaign imagery, categories, new arrivals, ranking, designer recommendations, events, lookbook content, and news without treating every module as equally dominant.

Main visual Lead with the brand mood.

Large campaign imagery creates an immediate, immersive entry before the user reaches denser commerce content.

Category Make collection entry visual.

Women and Men category banners separate major shopping paths while reinforcing collection imagery and brand tone.

New arrivals Reduce noise around new products.

A simpler product grid increases focus on new merchandise and creates a more direct route into product detail.

Ranking Surface popular products quickly.

A ranking module gives shoppers a fast way to scan high-interest products without adding another dense catalogue block.

Image treatment

The long desktop capture is intentionally shown at a restrained width to preserve legibility. Elsewhere in this case study, the same source is cropped to surface specific design decisions instead of forcing recruiters to inspect one oversized full-page screenshot.

06 · Mobile Experience

Mobile was designed as its own shopping rhythm.

The mobile experience prioritizes brand impact, product scanning, ranking, recommendations, editorial content, and persistent commerce navigation in a narrower interaction space.

Responsive decision

Instead of reproducing the desktop page at a smaller scale, the mobile layout reduces simultaneous choices, increases visual focus, and keeps key navigation and commerce actions within easy reach.

07 · Product Discovery

A calmer catalogue for scanning a broad collection.

The product-list experience gives each item enough space to read as fashion merchandise while keeping categories and sorting visible. The wide grid is presented close to its source resolution so product detail remains readable.

08 · Product Detail

Reworking the product page from a long information stack into a clearer purchase journey.

The legacy PDP already contained the essential commerce information, but purchase controls, recommendation content, brand imagery, specifications, care details, reviews, and policy content were distributed across a very long page. The renewal reorganized that hierarchy so key purchase decisions stay easier to scan while deeper product content remains available below.

01
Purchase hierarchy

Primary product media, price, options, and purchase actions are treated as the first decision layer instead of competing with deeper content.

02
Content sequencing

Brand imagery and product storytelling remain part of the experience, but supporting specifications and care information are structured as secondary detail.

03
Decision support

Size guidance, related products, and product information are organized to support comparison and confidence without overloading the top of the page.

UX rationale

The goal was not to shorten the page at any cost. Fashion commerce still benefits from rich product storytelling. The redesign instead separates immediate purchase decisions from deeper information so customers can move at their own pace.

09 · Brand Content

One commerce system, multiple editorial jobs.

Beyond product templates, the site supports recurring content with different roles: event banners connect promotions and recommended products, lookbooks pair seasonal styling with shopping, and news cards make brand activity easier to scan at a glance.

11 · Measurement & Validation

The next iteration should be measured around discovery, engagement, and purchase behaviour.

This case study does not attribute unverified conversion gains to the redesign. Instead, these are the signals I would use to evaluate whether the renewed hierarchy is actually reducing friction.

Discovery Homepage → PLP / PDP click-through

Which homepage modules and category entry points most effectively move users into shopping?

Engagement Scroll depth + module interaction

Do users continue into Ranking, Lookbook, Event, and News, or does attention drop before those modules?

Product PLP → PDP progression

Can customers scan the catalogue efficiently and reach relevant product-detail pages without unnecessary friction?

Commerce PDP option / cart completion

Where do users hesitate between product information, option selection, and adding an item to cart?

Validation plan

Pair behavioural analytics with short task-based usability sessions on desktop and mobile: find a new product, compare items, locate a seasonal collection, and complete a purchase-intent task. The findings would determine which modules should be promoted, simplified, or reordered in the next release.
