Owned Brand · E-commerce Redesign · ANEW GOLF

Rebuilding ANEW GOLF
for a sharper digital brand experience.

ANEW GOLF is an in-house golf fashion brand operated by our company. I worked on the e-commerce renewal across UI/UX, responsive design, and front-end implementation — reshaping the storefront so brand storytelling and shopping could work together more clearly.

TL;DR

Renewed an existing owned-brand commerce experience — clarified the homepage hierarchy, strengthened responsive product discovery, and extended the same visual system across product, editorial, event, and news content in a live Cafe24 store.

01 · Context

An owned brand needed a storefront that could evolve with the business.

Methodology note

This case study documents a real production redesign. It is grounded in the existing store structure, live product/content requirements, responsive implementation constraints, and day-to-day commerce operations. Design rationale is separated from outcomes that would require dedicated analytics or research validation.

ANEW GOLF already had an active e-commerce site, but the experience had accumulated many promotional, merchandising, and editorial modules over time. The renewal was an opportunity to make the brand feel more current while giving customers a clearer path through product discovery.

Because this is our own brand, the work also had to support ongoing seasonal launches, product updates, campaigns, events, lookbooks, and news — not just a one-time visual refresh.

02 · Before / After

The renewal changed both the visual language and the content hierarchy.

The previous store relied on a conventional commerce structure with many similarly weighted modules. The renewed experience uses larger editorial moments, a more deliberate rhythm, and clearer transitions between brand storytelling and shopping.

What changed

The redesign was not a surface-level reskin. It reorganized how the homepage prioritizes campaign content, category discovery, products, rankings, recommendations, editorial content, events, and news.

03 · Evidence & Constraints

Design decisions came from the realities of an operating commerce brand.

Because ANEW GOLF is an owned brand, the redesign had to work inside a real content pipeline: frequent product launches, campaign updates, editorial assets, promotions, and Cafe24 implementation constraints.

Observed Store Structure

Many content types were competing on one long homepage.

Product modules, campaign imagery, editorial content, rankings, events, and news all needed visibility. The key design problem was hierarchy rather than simply adding more content.

Operational Requirement

The system had to survive seasonal change.

The brand continuously updates products and campaigns, so recurring modules needed reusable patterns instead of one-off layouts that would become expensive to maintain.

Platform Constraint

Design had to translate into a live Cafe24 environment.

Responsive behaviour, content modules, and front-end decisions were shaped by the production CMS/theme, not only by what looked ideal in a static design file.

04 · Design Direction

Editorial enough to feel like fashion. Structured enough to shop.

The redesign balances large brand imagery with predictable commerce patterns. Rather than letting every module compete at the same visual weight, the homepage uses a clearer rhythm: campaign impact first, product discovery next, then supporting editorial and brand content.

05 · Homepage

A modular homepage with clearer hierarchy and pacing.

The renewed homepage connects campaign imagery, categories, new arrivals, ranking, designer recommendations, events, lookbook content, and news without treating every module as equally dominant.

Image treatment

The long desktop capture is intentionally shown at a restrained width to preserve legibility. Elsewhere in this case study, the same source is cropped to surface specific design decisions instead of forcing recruiters to inspect one oversized full-page screenshot.

06 · Mobile Experience

Mobile was designed as its own shopping rhythm.

The mobile experience prioritizes brand impact, product scanning, ranking, recommendations, editorial content, and persistent commerce navigation in a narrower interaction space.

Responsive decision

Instead of reproducing the desktop page at a smaller scale, the mobile layout reduces simultaneous choices, increases visual focus, and keeps key navigation and commerce actions within easy reach.

07 · Product Discovery

A calmer catalogue for scanning a broad collection.

The product-list experience gives each item enough space to read as fashion merchandise while keeping categories and sorting visible. The wide grid is presented close to its source resolution so product detail remains readable.

08 · Product Detail

Keep purchase decisions clear while supporting deeper product storytelling.

The product detail page combines product media, options, pricing, recommendations, campaign imagery, size information, care details, and supporting policy content within one long commerce flow.

UX focus

Primary purchase information stays concentrated near the top, while deeper brand and specification content remains available further down the page. The hierarchy supports both quick decision-making and customers who want more product context.

09 · Brand Content

One commerce system, multiple editorial jobs.

Beyond product templates, the site supports recurring content with different purposes: lookbooks for seasonal storytelling, event pages for campaigns, and news for brand activity and partnerships.

11 · Measurement & Validation

The next iteration should be measured around discovery, engagement, and purchase behaviour.

The portfolio does not attribute unverified conversion gains to the redesign. Instead, these are the signals I would use to evaluate whether the renewed hierarchy is actually reducing friction.

Discovery Homepage → PLP / PDP click-through

Which homepage modules and category entry points most effectively move users into shopping?

Engagement Scroll depth + module interaction

Do users continue into Ranking, Lookbook, Event, and News, or does attention drop before those modules?

Product PLP → PDP progression

Can customers scan the catalogue efficiently and reach relevant product-detail pages without unnecessary friction?

Commerce PDP option / cart completion

Where do users hesitate between product information, option selection, and adding an item to cart?

Validation plan

Pair behavioural analytics with short task-based usability sessions on desktop and mobile: find a new product, compare items, locate a seasonal collection, and complete a purchase-intent task. The findings would determine which modules should be promoted, simplified, or reordered in the next release.
